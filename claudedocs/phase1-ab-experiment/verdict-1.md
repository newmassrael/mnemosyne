# Verdict — Saltglass A/B Judging

Judged per the brief: each matched world-line read in full, both copies, rubric
answered per pair. Copies carry no scene ids, so citations use scene titles.

A note that applies across pairs: every B copy ends with an orphaned italic
fragment — a partial sentence beginning mid-clause and closing with a stray
asterisk (confront-destroy B: "is true and the truth is unprovable; Maren
keeps her number, twenty steps from her name.*"; confront-reveal B: "pays in
full, Caul falls at the assizes, and the grave gets its name back.*"; quiet B:
"dangerous word said aloud on the island; the boy is a witness, not a
suspect; the stone is allowed to be wrong.*"). These read as leftover synopsis
or outline lines that survived into the reading copy. I count each as one
continuity/production error in its pair below.

---

## Pair 1: confront-destroy

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 2 (both minor):**

1. Day-count drift in the trunk. Mara lands "two days after the storm" (What
   the Storm Left; body found at first light on the twelfth), and meets Liss
   that same day — yet in What Liss Keeps, narrated the same day, "the lie
   was four days old." It is at most two to three days old. The same loose
   count recurs in Church-Dry ("four days on you walked round that pool"),
   where the Inventory walk-through was two or three days back.
2. End-game compression. Caul insists throughout on "Friday's packet"; yet
   between the burning and Friday morning the Lethbridge papers must arrive
   "on the next packet," and a four-hour inquest must sit and return
   misadventure (Pages Tear / The Weather Turns). It can be squared, barely,
   but the calendar is never shown doing it.

**Copy B — 2 (one hard, one minor):**

1. The terminal orphan fragment after The One True Thing (see headnote) — a
   hard copy defect: the book ends mid-sentence on text belonging to no scene.
2. In The Catechism, Relocked, Liss says "The storm tears ragged — you told
   me so yourself." Mara never says this to her on the page; in Catechism she
   says only "You tore it clean." The callback points at a conversation that
   does not exist in the copy.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction

**A: 4/5.** The forensic chain is immaculate — glass sliver to glazing rack to
gallery geometry (The Tower, Saltglass); the May mantle order to the April
"rehearsal"; the coin burn to the vaporizer; the sea-pinks to a living woman
wearing a dead name, paid off twice (Sea-Pinks for Maren Kolb; the closing
image of flowers "from a hand he never once thought about"). The signature
scene ("her hand did not shake, and she minded that most of all") is a
genuine payoff of ten years of certificates. Two threads dangle: Tobin, who
was about to confess in Two Years of Evenings, simply disappears from the
last three scenes; and the eight living refugees in the pilchard palace are
resolved only by implication ("Merrick's balance he wrote off").

**B: 5/5.** Everything planted is spent. The dry boots (The Lamp Room,
Saltglass, Catechism) are laced back on in Ash — "nothing left for the boots
to tell." The four unfinished scraper strokes pay off in the epilogue ("the
glass has not been let go gray since"). The sooted mantles against the dues
book make the lamp itself the document. The fee book's ruled line, the
memorandum filed "where paper goes to be safe from being read," Tam's exile,
the two graves keeping counsel — every character and prop is accounted for,
and the burning is given its bitterest possible meaning: the ash scattered
seaward is "the nearest thing to a delivery her father's promise would ever
get."

### 3. Stakes and tension

**A: 5/5.** The noose over Tobin is explicit and credible ("They hang men for
less, and call the quickness mercy"); Caul escalates visibly — witnesses
shipped by night, the body buried before breakfast, the Third Paper scene
where he sells Mara a dead man. The grate scene is run in real time, line by
burning line, and hurts accordingly.

**B: 4/5.** Quieter by design. Caul's threat to Mara ("packets get missed on
this coast," Jetsam) and Tam's disintegration carry it, and Ash is superb;
but The Width of the Possible concedes the outcome in advance ("She had done
the arithmetic every night for a week. It came out the same in every
column"), which drains the final scenes of suspense in exchange for
fatalism.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability

**A: 5/5.** Knowledge is scrupulously tracked: Mara's deductions are all
evidence-backed; Caul deduces his son's involvement only from Tobin's slip
("She—" one word too late); Liss's information about Tobin traces to two
years of evenings. Caul's "your own eyes have seen it, I think" about the
store-room is a guess and framed as one.

**B: 4/5.** Almost as clean, with one wobble: in The Width of the Possible
Mara's arithmetic asserts "an agent already dissolving like sugar and a
master gone foreign" — facts she has no channel for in this line, since Caul
cut the cable in Housekeeping and her only mail went outbound. It is framed
as projection, but it is suspiciously accurate projection.

### 5. Forced choice: **B**

B is the truer telling of CONFRONT + DESTROY. A's confrontation half is the
stronger of the two (Cards on the Desk and The Third Paper are better
antagonist scenes than anything in B), but A flinches at the destruction: the
ending pockets a ring and a letter and promises a future reckoning, which
converts the tragedy into a deferral. B commits — the memorandum is filed
unread, the agent dissolves, and the loss is made total and thematic, with
the catechism ("the one true thing in everything she says") closing the loop
opened in the first paragraph. B's mystery mechanics are also more organic:
the salt on the glass, the scraper strokes, and the sooted mantles make the
death and the fraud grow from one object, where A must import a pane in a
carpentered frame. B's epilogue resolves every named character; A leaves
Tobin and the eight living refugees hanging. B's terminal fragment is an
ugly production blemish and its tension sags late, but judged as a story of
this world-line, B is the more complete and the more honest one.

## Pair 2: confront-reveal

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 3 (one hard, two minor):**

1. Hard contradiction at the branch joint. In The Grate and the Glass, Liss
   "sat at the watch desk and unpicked the hem. The page came out warm from
   being carried." In the next scene, Through the Pool, "She took the page
   from her coat — not unpicked; torn free, threads hanging." Same night,
   same page: it cannot be both deliberately unpicked at the desk and torn
   free in haste at Mara's door.
2. The light is abandoned. Liss is the sole keeper ("from sunset to sunrise
   the lamp had to be wound, fed, trimmed," What Liss Keeps), yet a little
   before midnight she is down at the Halyard giving a long confession
   (Through the Pool) with no one in the tower — in a story whose whole
   spine is that the light is never left. Never acknowledged.
3. The trunk's "lie was four days old" day-count drift (as in Pair 1).

**Copy B — 3 (two hard, one minor):**

1. The terminal orphan fragment after In Performance of Duty (see headnote).
2. Date arithmetic. The trunk fixes the mother as "twelve years dead"
   (M. Carrow), her stone reads "TAKEN BY THE SEA, 1913," and the letter says
   "Twelve years I have kept it burning" — implying the present is 1925. The
   amended register in In Performance of Duty reads "TAKEN BY THE SEA 1913;
   GIVEN BACK 1923": ten years, not twelve. One of the two figures is wrong,
   and the error lands on the story's most sentimental payoff line.
3. In The Last Offer, "for the first time in eleven days she stood in the
   harbor office in the cold." The reconstructable clock (Tuesday arrival,
   burial Friday, the page the next dawn) puts this nearer day six or seven.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction

**A: 5/5.** The richest payoff set of the six copies. The twice-handled 1907
ledger pays off when Mara splits the back board in front of witnesses; item
31 is opened exactly when it can do work; the sea-pinks resolve into Ana
Sorel and the ring goes home "R·S against her collarbone, 1903 against her
heart"; the survivors come down in daylight and the island's practised
not-looking is inverted; both certificates are rewritten as judgments
("died in defence of his light"). Nothing planted is wasted.

**B: 4/5.** The page, the fee book, the ruled line read "by exactly the wrong
people," and the scraper-strokes payoff (Liss on the gallery "finishing the
sentence") are all excellent. Two deductions: the horn button, the physical
proof that decides everything, is planted retroactively — Mara's note
"shoulder seam torn outward, button gone" is first disclosed in the same
scene that pays it off (The Button), so the reader never had the clue; and
the register amendment, a major payoff, carries the 1923/twelve-years error.

### 3. Stakes and tension

**A: 5/5.** The midnight confession is the world-line's high point and is
played for full cost ("the name came out steady, which cost what it cost");
Tobin surrenders an hour ahead of arrest; and the ending leaves real
jeopardy live — the autumn assize, "juries are made of fathers" — rather
than discharging it.

**B: 4/5.** The Last Offer is the best Caul scene in either copy ("the
kindliness simply stopped... the way a lamp stops when the gas is shut at
the main"). But the button defuses the trunk's central, carefully built
ambiguity — "she would spend the rest of her life deciding what that reach
had been" — with one convenient artifact, and after it nothing is at risk
for anyone the reader cares about; the inquest is a coronation.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability

**A: 5/5.** The chain holds: Liss's account of Tobin's midnight return traces
to the boathouse meetings; Mara's ledger gambit is built from her own stated
inference ("forgers keep originals"); Caul's foreknowledge is always shown
being acquired. The one soft spot — Liss asserting "Caul called the whole of
it off" — is traceable to Tobin and flagged as her framing.

**B: 5/5.** Clean throughout. Mara's question "What was in your hand when you
came down?" is earned by the seam note; the constables find the chest by
warrant; Liss's dawn arrival keeps her own counsel about where she stood
until she chooses to give it.

### 5. Forced choice: **A**

A is the better realization of CONFRONT + REVEAL, because in A the reveal is
a moral act with a price, and in B it is an evidentiary convenience. A's
climax belongs to Liss — soaked to the knee from going straight through the
pool she had been walking around all week, the wet hem answering the
church-dry hem of the opening accusation — and the cost of speaking
(Tobin's assize, unresolved on purpose) stays on the table to the last page.
B's reveal is followed almost immediately by a button that settles the
trunk's defining ambiguity from outside the characters, after which the
remaining scenes are procedure executed by a protagonist who cannot lose.
A's hard error (the unpicked/torn page) is a one-line prop slip at the
joint; B's hard errors — a synopsis fragment as the literal last line, and a
date contradiction sitting inside the register payoff — do more damage to
the reading experience. A also pays off a larger cast more completely. A,
clearly.

## Pair 3: quiet

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 3 (one hard, two minor):**

1. "There are two T.C.s on that quay" (The Inside Stair). There is one:
   Tobin Caul. The harbormaster is Garth Caul. The line only parses if it
   means "two Cauls," which is not what it says, and Mara's "the young one"
   implies an older T.C. who does not exist in this copy.
2. In The Price Per Head, Mara cites "the *Linnet*" to Caul — a name she can
   only know from item 31, which she secretly opened and is simultaneously
   wielding as sealed leverage ("Is addressed to an Examiner of Lights").
   Naming the Linnet tells Caul the seal is broken; the scene does not
   notice.
3. The trunk's "lie was four days old" day-count drift (as in Pair 1).

**Copy B — 2 (both hard):**

1. The terminal orphan fragment after The Receiver's Men (see headnote).
2. The same 1913 / "twelve years dead" / "GIVEN BACK BY THE SEA, 1923" date
   contradiction as in Pair 2 (M. Carrow, Stone, The Receiver's Men).

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction

**A: 4/5.** The bread arithmetic ("Flour was paper you could eat") into the
pilchard-palace stakeout into the graveside meeting with Ana is the best new
material in the line, and the ring, the slow-cut stone, and the letter
forwarded with one attached line ("the quiet inspection should begin at the
harbor office") all land. But the climactic bargain (The Price Per Head) is
dented by the two glitches above, and the half-sheet — the story's buried
treasure — is surrendered on demand rather than discovered, which is less a
payoff than a transaction.

**B: 5/5.** The quiet method is itself the planted thing, and it pays
everywhere: letters walked into the packet master's own hand; the day-book
read while pricing shawl-wool; the screen logged on a Fund inventory form
("said nothing else, which was the method of the month"); a warrant "raised
entirely on paper... There had been nothing for the island to refuse." The
"by hand" address on the dead man's letter pays off in the scene title and
act of By Hand — the best single scene in either quiet copy — and the
refrain about silence and money is spent precisely once, at the end.

### 3. Stakes and tension

**A: 4/5.** The stakeout carries real exposure, the living refugees give the
file human weight ("The count that mattered now was the living"), and the
bargain scene has teeth. The quiet road still bends back into one
confrontation, which keeps the temperature up.

**B: 3/5.** Deliberately low heat, and it costs. Caul never threatens, never
suspects, never learns he is in danger until the ensign clears the mole;
dramatic irony ("a man working a pump in a ship already flooded to the
orlop") substitutes for jeopardy. Mourners and By Hand supply feeling, but
for long stretches the only suspense is whether the post will arrive.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability

**A: 3/5.** The serious failure of the six copies: in The Price Per Head Mara
demands "the half-sheet — fourteen names, your pencil — into my hand, now."
She can know fourteen (Ana told her "eight of fourteen"), but the half-sheet
and the pencil are physical specifics established only in Caul's private
scene (The Harbormaster's Arithmetic); no channel carries them to her. Add
the Linnet slip and the "two T.C.s" muddle, all in the same stretch.

**B: 5/5.** The most disciplined knowledge-keeping in the set. Mara never
acts on anything she has not been shown acquiring; Liss never says where she
stood, and Mara "did not ask; the asking was a price the quiet way had never
once needed to pay" — the line makes the discipline explicit and thematic.

### 5. Forced choice: **B**

B wins the quiet world-line because B understands what "quiet" is for. Its
investigation is conducted entirely off-island, on paper, and the structure
embodies the premise: the island is never given a dangerous word to refuse,
and the arrest arrives as the logical terminus of silence rather than as a
negotiated settlement. A's quiet road, by contrast, terminates in a
confrontation scene that the branch label promised to avoid, and that scene
contains the pair's worst defects — a bluff built on unknowable physical
detail, a leverage play that contradicts itself, and the "two T.C.s" line.
A is warmer (the living refugees, the graveside scene) and tenser, and B's
date error and trailing fragment are real blemishes; but A's flaws are
load-bearing — they sit inside the climax and inside the heroine's
competence, which is the whole subject of this branch — while B's are
peripheral. B, with By Hand as the deciding scene.
