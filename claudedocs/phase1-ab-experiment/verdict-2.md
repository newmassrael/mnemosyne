# Verdict — Saltglass A/B, judged per matched world-line

Copies carry no scene ids; citations below use scene titles (and position where
helpful). "Trunk" means material shared by all three copies of that version.

A note that applies to every B copy: each one ends with a truncated italic
fragment after the final scene (confront-destroy B: "is true and the truth is
unprovable; Maren keeps her number…"; confront-reveal B: "pays in full, Caul
falls at the assizes…"; quiet B: "dangerous word said aloud on the island…").
These read as leaked outline/summary lines, not story text. The final scenes
themselves are complete, so no ending is lost, but the defect breaks the close
of every B copy and I have counted it as a continuity error each time.

---

## Pair 1: confront-destroy

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 3 noted:**
1. Lie-age arithmetic. Mara lands "two days after the storm" ("What the Storm
   Left") and interviews Liss the same day, yet in "What Liss Keeps" — the same
   day — "the lie was four days old." The lie began at dawn on the 12th; it can
   be at most two to three days old when she repeats it to Mara.
2. Tower height drift. "The Tower" puts the flags "sixty feet under the gallery
   rail"; in "Pages Tear" Liss speaks of spray "a hundred feet up in a gale."
   Dialogue exaggeration is arguable, but the numbers fight.
3. Unsourced knowledge spreading. In "Two Years of Evenings" Tobin says of the
   sealed letter "The whole town has it by now," when only Mara and Liss knew
   of its removal. Related loose thread: the *Periwinkle* carried "crew five"
   ("The Harbormaster's Arithmetic"), but only mate Veck and seaman Crey are
   ever accounted for ("The Stowaway"); the master and two hands vanish from
   the story.

**Copy B — 3 noted:**
1. The trailing truncated fragment after "The One True Thing" (see headnote).
2. The missing passengers. Fourteen deck fares were booked on the *Periwinkle*
   ("In Ballast," "Housekeeping"); the crew "got off in their own boat" ("The
   Packet from Harwick"); exactly one body comes ashore ("Stone"). The other
   twelve or thirteen passengers — drowned? landed? — are never accounted for
   anywhere in the copy, and an island that turns out "to the walls" for one
   unknown woman never wonders about a dozen more.
3. In "The Catechism, Relocked," Hannet "kept a tidy watch and slept a tidy
   sleep" while Liss burned the page at the lamp ("Ash"), yet over breakfast he
   reports having noticed "the lamp flaring in the small hours." A man
   established as asleep is also the witness to the flare.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction
- **A: 4.** The glass sliver → screen frame, the May mantle → April rehearsal,
  the coin burn → vaporizer, item 31 → the failed last card, and Caul's "third
  paper" all pay off cleanly; the bitterest payoff (Mara signing "the kindest
  lie available") is earned. Docked one point because the living refugees in
  the pilchard palace — heavily set up in "The Harbormaster's Arithmetic" and
  "Sea-Pinks for Maren Kolb" — are resolved only by a narratorial wink in the
  closing paragraphs ("from a hand he never once thought about"); Mara never
  finds them in this line.
- **B: 4.** The boots, the four scraper strokes, the carton, and the burning
  scene ("Ash" — Liss lacing on the boots afterward is the best single payoff
  image in either copy) are immaculate. Docked for the unaccounted passengers
  (a planted fourteen that never detonates) and for the appended fragment
  fouling the close.

### 3. Stakes and tension
- **A: 5.** The double bind — Liss must choose between her father's name and
  Tobin's neck while Caul builds a counter-story out of true facts — keeps two
  blades at her throat at once, and "The Grate and the Glass" / "What the Grate
  Was For" land it.
- **B: 4.** The page-burning sequence is as strong as A's, but the stakes ride
  almost entirely on the keeper's honor and Tam's safety; the deflated
  passenger count (one body, no questions) quietly lowers the storm's cost.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability
- **A: 4.** Mostly disciplined (Mara's deductions are all from observed facts;
  Caul never reveals what he can't know). Tobin's "the whole town has it by
  now" and his certainty that "she kept it burning the whole night" stretch
  what he could know.
- **B: 5.** Notably careful. Liss explicitly registers "lights, out past the
  shoal" as new information from Tam ("The Two Liars"); Mara never uses
  anything the reader saw in Caul-POV scenes; Caul's "no living soul on that
  tower beside him" is a lie he knows to be one. The only wobble is the
  Hannet flare noted above.

### 5. Forced choice: **A**

A wins on completeness of its world. Every soul in A is accounted for — eight
alive, six dead, each fate tracked — while B leaves a dozen booked passengers
unexplained and ends in appended garbage text. A's destroy-line also carries a
crueler, richer irony: Caul's frame-up is built entirely out of facts Mara can
prove, so the burned page destroys the only counter-paper, and Mara's closing
line about the one true sentence she signed cuts deeper than B's equivalent.
B has the better single scene ("Ash") and the stricter knowledge hygiene, and
on prose the two are close to even; but B's structural hole and broken close
cost it the pair.

## Pair 2: confront-reveal

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 3 noted:**
1. The same trunk lie-age error ("two days after the storm" vs "the lie was
   four days old" — "What the Storm Left" / "What Liss Keeps").
2. The same trunk loose crew accounting ("crew five," only Veck and Crey ever
   seen — "The Stowaway").
3. Time inflation at the close: "the island that had practised not-looking for
   a fortnight" ("What the Lamp Is For"), when the action spans roughly a week
   from a storm two days before Mara's Friday-deadline arrival. Also the trunk
   "whole town has it by now" line ("Two Years of Evenings").

**Copy B — 3 noted:**
1. The trailing truncated fragment after "In Performance of Duty."
2. The missing twelve-plus passengers again: the assizes charge "fourteen
   unlicensed passages" but no copy-internal accounting of the people; only
   M. Carrow's body ever surfaces or is asked after.
3. Retro-planted evidence: in "The Button," Mara reveals she "had spent an
   hour with the keeper's coat in the salt store, and had written in her book:
   *shoulder seam torn outward, button gone*." The body examination the reader
   was shown ("Saltglass") records forearms, skull, and scraper — no coat, no
   seam. The decisive clue is declared into the past rather than planted.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction
- **A: 5.** The fullest payoff cascade in all six copies. The dry-yard motif
  inverts perfectly (Liss arriving "soaked dark to the knee" — "Through the
  Pool" — pays off three separate setups at once); "forgers keep originals"
  pays off in the 1907 ledger's clean dust; the sea-pinks become Ana Sorel;
  the ring goes home; both certificates resolve both files.
- **B: 4.** The button is a wonderful concrete clue and the closing image —
  Liss finishing her father's four scraper strokes — is the best ending image
  of the pair. But the button's mating seam was never planted (above), and the
  fragment fouls the close.

### 3. Stakes and tension
- **A: 5.** The reveal costs something real: Tobin walks to Brask's door
  himself, and the ending honestly leaves him facing the autumn assize with no
  promised mercy. Caul's arrest, the survivors descending in daylight, and the
  inquest all pay tension that was banked earlier.
- **B: 5.** "The Last Offer" — the kindliness shutting off "the way a lamp
  stops when the gas is shut at the main" — is the strongest Caul beat in any
  copy, and the dawn handover of the page is earned.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability
- **A: 5.** The one place A could have cheated — Mara finding the half-sheet —
  is handled honestly: she reasons from shelf dust and her own earlier line,
  not from the Caul-POV scene the reader saw. Tobin's surrender needs no
  knowledge he lacks.
- **B: 5.** Liss's reasoning about why she cannot testify ("she could not say
  *only a reach* without saying where she stood to see it") is exactly right;
  Tam's account matches the girl's "grain to grain" without either knowing the
  other spoke. Clean throughout.

### 5. Forced choice: **A**

The closest pair, because B's confrontation scenes and final image are
superb. A takes it on payoff integrity: A's clinching evidence (the ledger
dust, the page, Pinn's freeboard, the frame) was all planted on the page
before it was used, while B's clinching physical clue — the torn shoulder
seam the button mates — is introduced retroactively at the moment of need,
which is the one sin a fair-play mystery cannot afford in its reveal line. A
also resolves more of its world (the living come down from the pilchard
palace; the warrant-list question is honestly left to lawyers) where B again
leaves most of its fourteen passengers unaccounted, and B again trails off
into a leaked fragment.

## Pair 3: quiet

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 2 noted:**
1. The trunk lie-age error ("What the Storm Left" / "What Liss Keeps").
2. "There are two T.C.s on that quay" ("The Inside Stair"). The harbormaster
   is Garth Caul — G.C., not T.C. The follow-up ("the young one") shows Mara
   means father and son Caul, but as written the line asserts an initial match
   the text never supports.

**Copy B — 3 noted:**
1. The trailing truncated fragment after "The Receiver's Men."
2. An assembly seam: "In Ballast" leaves the fee book "locked in the lower
   drawer" with M. Carrow's column open. "The Receiver's Men" then finds it
   in "the iron chest beneath the bed" with "one line ruled clean through
   M. Carrow" — but the scene establishing both (the move to the chest, the
   ruling of the line) does not exist in this copy; it belongs to the other
   world-lines. Within this linear copy, two load-bearing details appear from
   nowhere, and the narration even leans on the ruled line as the famous
   detail "the newspapers kept."
3. The missing passengers, as in every B copy: fourteen booked fares, one
   body, no further accounting.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction
- **A: 5.** The quiet route pays everything the trunk planted: the bread
  arithmetic ("Salt and Glass") leads to the pilchard palace; the sea-pinks
  lead to Ana and the ring goes home at the graveside; the half-sheet comes
  out of the 1907 ledger exactly where the reader saw it hidden; the page is
  read and returned ("Sew it in again"); and the letter to the Examiner is
  delivered with Mara's one attached line, keeping the reckoning alive past
  the bargain.
- **B: 4.** The single best branch-consistent payoff in any B copy is here:
  the page was hidden in the spare-mantle carton in scene one, and the quiet
  route's plain-sight inventory is precisely what finds it ("Inventory") — the
  method and the discovery are the same thing. Docked for the fee-book seam
  (its courtroom payoff rests on an unestablished event) and the fragment.

### 3. Stakes and tension
- **A: 4.** Quieter by design, but the stakes are the highest of any line —
  eight living people whose fate turns on what Mara signs — and "The Price
  Per Head" converts a week of patience into one genuinely tense bargaining
  scene. The trade-off is that Caul folds in a single scene once confronted.
- **B: 4.** The discipline is the pleasure — eleven days without "one
  dangerous word said aloud," the warrant "raised entirely on paper" — and
  the Receiver's cutter is a satisfying quiet climax. But with no living
  passengers at risk and Caul never truly cornered face to face, the line
  risks less than A's, and the unbroken procedural victory costs it some
  tension at the end.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability
- **A: 5.** The dangerous spot is handled well: Mara's demand for "the
  half-sheet — fourteen names, your pencil" could have been narrator-fed, but
  the copy shows her sources — Ana gives her "eight of fourteen" and the
  borrowed-names scheme at the Kolb stone ("The Lender's Due") — and she never
  names the ledger; Caul walks to it himself. Liss's "Nobody climbed"
  cracking only when Mara produces what the letter proves is consistent.
- **B: 5.** Scrupulous. Mara explicitly declines the one thing she cannot
  source ("What the girl knew, she did not have — and… would not ask"), and
  her closing claim to Liss carefully states only what the page itself says.

### 5. Forced choice: **A**

A again, and for the quiet line the margin is partly structural: a quiet-route
story lives or dies on the reader believing every step was available to the
investigator, and quiet-B's courtroom climax rests on two details (the iron
chest, the ruled line) that this copy never establishes — the seam shows. A's
quiet line is also simply playing for more: living people rather than a
posthumous correction, a morally priced bargain rather than a clean win, and
an ending that leaves the Examiner's letter ticking instead of tying
everything in green ribbon. B's restraint is admirable and its
inventory-finds-the-page beat is the cleverest single plot mechanic in either
version, but the assembly seam, the orphaned passengers, and the trailing
fragment are all continuity costs A does not pay.

## Summary

| World-line | A errors | B errors | Setup/payoff | Stakes | Knowledge | Choice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| confront-destroy | 3 | 3 | A 4 / B 4 | A 5 / B 4 | A 4 / B 5 | **A** |
| confront-reveal | 3 | 3 | A 5 / B 4 | A 5 / B 5 | A 5 / B 5 | **A** |
| quiet | 2 | 3 | A 5 / B 4 | A 4 / B 4 | A 5 / B 5 | **A** |

A sweeps, but narrowly and not on prose — B's sentence-level craft and its
point-of-view knowledge discipline are at least A's equal, and B owns several
of the best individual scenes ("Ash," "The Last Offer," the inventory finding
the page). A wins on whole-world bookkeeping: every planted character and
object in A is accounted for in every line, while every B copy carries the
same two systemic defects — twelve-plus booked passengers who vanish from the
story, and a truncated non-story fragment appended after the final scene —
plus one line-specific seam each where a branch was assembled without its
supporting scene.
