# Verdict — Saltglass A/B judging

Judged per matched world-line, both linear copies read in full. Scene
citations use the section titles carried in the copies.

---

## Pair 1: confront-destroy

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 3 noticed**

1. **Unpaid narrator promise (moderate).** "Sea-Pinks for Maren Kolb" ends:
   "It would be days before she learned she had just, for the first time,
   written down the name of a living soul." In this world-line Mara never
   learns it. She never finds the half-sheet ("I never found your third
   paper" — "The Weather Turns"), never meets the survivors, and leaves the
   island with the sea-pinks unexplained. The promise is made and never
   kept in this line.
2. **Name-list position (minor/ambiguous).** "The Harbormaster's
   Arithmetic" quotes the half-sheet as "*Maren Kolb. Anders Tregale.
   Sefton Quy.*" — Kolb apparently heading the list — while "The Weather
   Turns" has "*Maren Kolb* third from the top." Defensible only if the
   earlier quote was not the top of the list, which nothing signals.
3. **Tower height drift (minor, in dialogue).** "The Tower" establishes the
   flags "sixty feet under the gallery rail"; in "Pages Tear" Liss says
   spray flies "a hundred feet up in a gale." Character exaggeration is
   possible, but the numbers sit oddly together.

**Copy B — 3 noticed**

1. **The boots are in two places (moderate).** In "Catechism" Mara fetches
   the dry boots from the tower's stair foot and puts them on the cottage
   table in front of Liss. Two scenes later ("Ash"), Liss, descending the
   tower, "stopped at the stair foot where her boots stood — dry through,
   soles pale, neat against the wall — and put them on," with no account of
   their return. The phrasing ("neat against the wall") asserts they never
   moved.
2. **Corrupted final lines (moderate).** The copy ends with a fragment that
   begins mid-sentence and terminates in a stray asterisk: "is true and the
   truth is unprovable; Maren keeps her number, twenty steps from her
   name.*" This is non-story text appended after the story's actual closing
   paragraph.
3. **Watch discipline wobble (minor).** "The Page" has Hannet keeping "a
   tidy watch" and sleeping "a tidy sleep" simultaneously while Liss burns
   the page in the working lantern at midnight; "The Catechism, Relocked"
   then has him noticing the flare. Survivable, but the keeper-on-watch
   asleep at the lamp is glossed rather than explained.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction

- **A: 4/5.** The torn page, the dry hem, the coin burn, the empty glazing
  slot, and the frame's geometry all pay off cleanly, and the burn scene
  ("What the Grate Was For") is the payoff of the whole hem motif. But the
  survivor thread (eight people alive above Seal Cove) is set up at length
  in "The Harbormaster's Arithmetic" and simply abandoned, and the
  "living soul" promise never lands (see error 1).
- **B: 5/5.** Nearly every planted detail discharges: the boots set at the
  stair foot in scene one are the lever of "Catechism"; the sooted mantles
  pay off the requisition-book anomaly; the receipt in the hem pays off the
  "where travelling women bank" line; the catechism sentence ("the light
  never went dark") is set up as the one true thing and closes the book as
  exactly that. The mother-revelation (M. Carrow = Maren Carrow Voss) is
  seeded in three separate documents before it detonates.

### 3. Stakes and tension

- **A: 5/5.** Three certificates' worth of pressure — Tobin's neck, the
  Voss name and benefit, the buried refugees — and Caul's counter-moves
  ("What a Careful Man Does," "Thursday Come Early") keep tightening the
  screws. The Caul/Mara duels are genuinely tense.
- **B: 5/5.** Slightly slower to ignite, but the stakes compound terribly:
  a live boy against a dead father's hand, and then the mother — Liss
  burning the only proof while her own mother lies under "Unknown" twenty
  steps from her stone is as high as the stakes get in any copy.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability

- **A: 4/5.** Mostly scrupulous. One wobble: in "What a Careful Man Does"
  Tobin tells his father "She kept it burning the whole night" before he
  and Liss have spoken since the storm ("Two Years of Evenings" is
  explicitly "the first time since the storm"). It's inferable — keeper
  dead, light burning, only Liss left — but it's stated as knowledge.
- **B: 5/5.** Exemplary. Liss acts only on what she saw from behind the
  clockwork and says so ("I saw a reach. That's all I own" is the
  confront-reveal phrasing, but the same discipline governs here); Mara's
  deductions are each traced to a document or an object; Caul's blind spot
  about M. Carrow is dramatized rather than violated ("Housekeeping").

### 5. Forced choice: **B**

B wins this world-line. Its destruction ending is better motivated: the
page Liss burns ("2.40am. T. Brod up from harbor… Refused") genuinely puts
a living boy on the tower minutes before the fall, so destroying it is the
rational act of the person we've watched, whereas A's Liss burns a page
that partly exculpates Tobin (sent off at dusk) on panic logic the text
itself half-concedes. B's forensic chain — boots, soot, draft, receipt —
is tighter, its knowledge boundaries are cleaner, and the mother
revelation gives the burning a second, crueler cost (her mother keeps her
number) that A's version has no equivalent for. A is the cleaner-edged
manuscript — B's misplaced boots and the garbage fragment after its final
line are real blemishes — but A also carries an abandoned survivor plot
and a narrator promise this branch never repays. As a story experienced
in this world-line, B is the more complete and the more devastating.

## Pair 2: confront-reveal

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 2 noticed**

1. **Off-by-one chronology (minor).** "Item 31" dates the 1907 ledger "dead
   these sixteen years" (implying 1923), while "Sea-Pinks for Maren Kolb"
   makes the 1894 drowning "thirty years" past (implying 1924). Round-number
   speech can absorb it, but the two figures don't quite meet.
2. **Unbriefed knowledge (very minor).** In "Item 31" Brask responds
   "April. The mantle." — the mantle anomaly was Mara's private ledger find;
   no scene shows her briefing him. Plausibly happened offstage.

**Copy B — 3 noticed**

1. **Explicit year contradiction (clear).** Liss's mother is "twelve years
   dead" ("M. Carrow"), her stone reads "TAKEN BY THE SEA, 1913" ("Stone"),
   the letter says "Twelve years I have kept it burning," and the stone
   "had waited twelve years to be wrong" — all pointing to 1925. The
   amended register in "In Performance of Duty" then reads "GIVEN BACK
   1923." 1913 to 1923 is ten years, not twelve.
2. **Corrupted final lines (moderate).** The copy ends with a mid-sentence
   fragment and stray asterisk: "pays in full, Caul falls at the assizes,
   and the grave gets its name back.*" — non-story text after the true
   ending.
3. **Retroactive setup (minor, shading into payoff critique).** "The
   Button" turns on Mara having "spent an hour with the keeper's coat in
   the salt store" and noted "shoulder seam torn outward, button gone" —
   an examination never shown or mentioned until the moment it's needed.
   Not a contradiction, but the decisive physical evidence is back-filled.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction

- **A: 5/5.** The strongest payoff architecture of all six copies. The
  flooded yard / dry hem motif, built across four scenes, pays off when
  Liss arrives "soaked dark to the knee… I'm done being dry" ("Through the
  Pool"). The 1907 ledger handled in "Cards on the Desk" pays off when
  Mara splits its back board; the sea-pinks pay off in living survivors;
  "forgers keep originals" is both a line and a plot mechanism. Even the
  inventory's item 31 numbering returns as a chapter title.
- **B: 4/5.** The page, the receipt, the ruled line in the fee book, and
  the unfinished scraper strokes (paid off beautifully by Liss "finishing
  the sentence" on the gallery) all land. Docked for the button — the hinge
  of the legal resolution — arriving without prior setup.

### 3. Stakes and tension

- **A: 5/5.** The reveal costs everyone something: Tobin walks to Brask's
  door himself and still faces the autumn assize with no promised mercy;
  the survivors step into daylight under warrant-list names; the island has
  to look at the tower at last. Consequence survives the resolution.
- **B: 4/5.** Tense through "The Last Offer" (the kindliness shutting off
  "like a lamp… shut at the main" is the best Caul beat in either copy),
  but the resolution is comparatively frictionless: the coroner clears
  everyone sympathetic, Tam is a witness not a defendant, and the ending
  tidies itself.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability

- **A: 4/5.** One soft spot: Liss asserts Tobin "went back up at midnight
  on his own — to spare his father the doing of it" ("Through the Pool").
  She knew the tread and the timing; the motive is her construction stated
  as fact, and the boathouse scene didn't supply it.
- **B: 5/5.** The story is practically about epistemic limits: Liss refuses
  to characterize the reach she saw; Tam's account matches hers "grain to
  grain" precisely because neither knew the other witnessed; Mara's case is
  assembled document by document. No one acts on anything they couldn't
  hold.

### 5. Forced choice: **A**

A takes this world-line. The reveal branch lives or dies on whether the
chain of disclosure feels earned, and A's chain is the best sustained
piece of plotting in the whole set: Liss chooses to walk through the
standing water she has detoured around for the entire story, and that
single image converts four scenes of forensic setup into character
action; the half-sheet then comes out of a ledger the reader watched Caul
handle. B's version is moving and procedurally satisfying, but its
resolution leans on a horn button whose setup is invented in the same
scene that spends it, it carries a flat date contradiction (twelve
years / 1913 / 1923), and its final line is corrupted text. A also keeps
consequence alive — Tobin still faces the assize, the survivors face the
warrant lists — where B closes every account at par. A's only real costs
are an off-by-one year and one unbriefed remark of Brask's; on craft and
on weight, it is the better telling here.

## Pair 3: quiet

### 1. Continuity errors

**Copy A — 3 noticed**

1. **The bread arithmetic overshoots the calendar (moderate).** In "The
   Pilchard Palace," Tansy walks the basket up "like a woman who had done
   it nine mornings." The survivors landed the night of the storm; Mara
   arrived two days after it, and this stakeout falls roughly five or six
   mornings post-storm on the copy's own timeline. Nine mornings don't fit.
2. **"Two T.C.s on that quay" (probable error).** In "The Inside Stair"
   Mara says "There are two T.C.s on that quay… the young one." Only Tobin
   Caul carries those initials; his father is Garth. If the line means "two
   Cauls," the initials don't say so; as written it gestures at a second
   T.C. the story never supplies.
3. **Bluff or leak (minor, knowledge-adjacent).** In "The Price Per Head"
   Mara demands "the half-sheet — fourteen names, your pencil." The count
   she has from Ana; that the list is a pencil half-sheet she has from
   nowhere — it exists only in Caul's POV scene. Readable as a bluff that
   happens to be exactly right, but the text doesn't mark it as one.

**Copy B — 3 noticed**

1. **Explicit year contradiction (clear).** Same as confront-reveal B:
   mother "twelve years dead," stone dated 1913, register amended "GIVEN
   BACK BY THE SEA, 1923" ("The Receiver's Men"). Ten years, not twelve.
2. **Corrupted final lines (moderate).** The copy ends mid-sentence with a
   stray asterisk: "dangerous word said aloud on the island; the boy is a
   witness, not a suspect; the stone is allowed to be wrong.*"
3. **Orphaned cause (soft).** "The Receiver's Men" finds the fee book in
   "the iron chest beneath the bed" with "one line ruled clean through
   M. Carrow." In this world-line the scene that produced both facts
   (Caul's "Housekeeping," triggered by a confrontation that never happens
   here) does not exist; the chest and the ruled line arrive as
   established facts without any on-page cause. The narrator can know it,
   but the beat is borrowed from a road not taken.

### 2. Setup/payoff satisfaction

- **A: 5/5.** The quiet method generates its own payoffs: the flour
  arithmetic ("flour was paper you could eat") leads to the pilchard
  palace; the sea-pinks lead to Ana at the graveside; the sealed letter,
  weighed before it is spent, becomes both Liss's absolution and the
  delayed fuse under Caul ("the quiet inspection should begin at the
  harbor office"). The dangling "living soul" promise from the shared
  opening is paid in full here.
- **B: 4/5.** Elegant: the page surfaces from the mantle carton exactly
  where the opening scene left it — the branch logic is airtight — and the
  "paper answers paper" thesis culminates in a warrant "raised entirely on
  paper." Docked for the ruled-line payoff arriving without its setup
  scene, and for a resolution that pays out almost without resistance.

### 3. Stakes and tension

- **A: 5/5.** The quiet road discovers something that transforms the
  problem: eight living people whose fate hangs on whatever Mara
  certifies. Her bargain — silence for lives, with the Examiner letter
  held back as a slow weapon — is morally expensive in a way the text
  makes you feel. Tension holds to the gangway.
- **B: 3/5.** Beautifully controlled, but the cost of control is jeopardy.
  Caul never knows he is hunted; Mara is never threatened (the one threat
  scene, "Jetsam," belongs to the confront branch and is absent here);
  the climax is a cutter arriving with paperwork. Dramatic irony carries
  it, suspense mostly doesn't.

### 4. Character-knowledge believability

- **A: 4/5.** Two wobbles: Liss asserts Tobin's midnight motive ("to spare
  his father the doing of it") with no boathouse scene in this branch to
  source it, and Mara's pencil-half-sheet specificity (above). Otherwise
  the surveillance chapters are scrupulous — Mara only ever acts on what
  she has seen from the furze or read in a book.
- **B: 5/5.** The branch's whole conceit is knowledge discipline — Mara
  deliberately declines to learn what Liss knows ("she did not need it,
  and would not ask") and builds the case entirely from documents that
  never touched the island. No one oversteps.

### 5. Forced choice: **A**

A takes the quiet world-line. The test of a "quiet" branch is whether
patience discovers something confrontation would have scattered, and A's
does: the stakeout finds the living, and from that moment the file stops
being about the dead and the certificate becomes a weapon pointed at
eight breathing people — the richest moral situation in any of the six
copies, resolved in a bargain that costs Mara visibly and leaves Caul a
delayed reckoning rather than an acquittal. B executes its
paper-only procedure with real elegance and stricter knowledge hygiene,
but it pays for that purity in stakes: no one sympathetic is ever in
danger after the midpoint, the antagonist never gets to push back, and
the copy carries the flat 1913/1923 date error, an effect (the ruled
line, the iron chest) whose cause exists only in a sibling branch, and a
corrupted final line. A's errors (nine mornings, the second T.C.) are
real but local; its story is larger.

## Summary

| World-line | Choice | Margin |
|---|---|---|
| confront-destroy | **B** | clear — better-motivated destruction, stronger payoff lattice, despite boots error and corrupted tail |
| confront-reveal | **A** | clear — earned reveal chain and surviving consequence vs. back-filled button, date error |
| quiet | **A** | moderate — discovery that raises stakes vs. elegant but frictionless procedure |

Cross-cutting observations: every B copy ends with a truncated,
asterisk-terminated fragment of non-story text, and two of the three B
copies carry the same 1913/"twelve years"/1923 date contradiction —
recurring defects that cost B in two pairs. Conversely, A's shared
opening plants a survivor plot and a narrator promise ("the name of a
living soul") that only some branches repay; in confront-destroy A both
dangle, which cost A that pair.
